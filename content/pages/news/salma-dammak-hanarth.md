[member/salma-dammak] has been awarded a Hanarth Fonds fellowship for her project, 'Becoming an implementation expert: translating AI for pathology into the laboratory and the operating room'. The Hanarth Fonds aims to promote and enhance the use of artificial intelligence and machine learning to improve the diagnosis, treatment, and outcomes for cancer patients, with a particular focus on rare cancers.
 
During her Hanarth fellowship, she will learn how to transform pathology AI models from scientific findings to clinically usable tools. To learn these skills, she will focus on a clinically impactful application: nodal assessment for head and neck cancer, which is a heterogeneous group of individually rare cancers that together form the seventh most common cancer group worldwide. First, she will translate a model for head and neck cancer node assessment in the pathology lab, learning from Radboudumc’s cutting-edge work on automating nodal assessment in breast cancer.
 
Her fellowship will be supervised by [member/jeroen-van-der-laak], with [Robert Takes](https://www.radboudumc.nl/en/people/robert-takes) as the surgical oncologist and [Ilse van Engen-van Grunsven](https://www.radboudumc.nl/personen/ilse-van-engen-van-grunsven) as the subspecialized pathologist supporting the fellowship project. She will also complete three international internships where she will learn from three world-leading experts in pathology AI translation: Junya Fukuoka (University, Nagasaki, Japan), Mattias Rantalainen (Karolinska Institutet, Sweden), and Solène-Florence Kammerer-Jacquet (Université de Rennes, France).
 
The research project in combination with the world-class expertise of the fellowship mentorship team will help Salma gain the skills she needs to become an AI translation expert who can transform research insights into patient impact.

Read more about the grant on the [Hanarth Fonds announcement](https://www.hanarthfonds.nl/en-2026-call).
