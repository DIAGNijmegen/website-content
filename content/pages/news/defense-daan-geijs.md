On Wednesday, October 7th at 16:30, [member/daan-geijs] successfully defended his [PhD thesis](https://repository.ubn.ru.nl/handle/2066/335224) titled 'Dealing with the wave: Automating skin cancer assessment'.

Skin cancer is the most common cancer worldwide: one in six people in the Netherlands develops basal cell carcinoma, the mildest type. Yet every excised lesion ends up with a pathologist, and this diagnosis forms the largest workload in any pathology laboratory. Daan's thesis shows that artificial intelligence (AI) recognizes basal cell carcinoma at the level of dermatopathologists. It also holds up during surgery, while the patient is still on the operating table, and in several international hospitals whose tissue the system had never seen. Such a system saves precious time in overburdened care by filtering these cases out of the daily stream of diagnoses. Does the AI replace the pathologist? No. The aim is to take over routine work, leaving the pathologist time for more complex cases. Whether daily practice bears that out is the next step, and with it the hope of healthcare that stays available and affordable.
