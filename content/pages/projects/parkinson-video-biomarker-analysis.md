## Problem description
Motor symptoms in Parkinson’s disease are commonly assessed using the Movement Disorder Society Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS). Although widely used, this assessment depends on visual scoring by clinicians and can therefore be subjective, time-consuming and sensitive to differences between raters. Recent work has shown that video recordings can be used to derive objective and clinically interpretable measurements of motor performance. A recently developed computer vision method for the finger-tapping test quantifies characteristics such as hypokinesia, bradykinesia, sequence effect and hesitation-halts, and can also estimate MDS-UPDRS severity from video [1]. However, these analysis methods are not yet available as a complete and practical workflow for clinicians and researchers. Recording suitable videos, analysing them, interpreting the measurements and comparing assessments still require separate steps and technical expertise.

## Aim
This collaborative project between RTC AI and the Parkinson research group aims to develop an all-in-one application for objective video-based assessment of Parkinsonian motor symptoms. The application will build on existing AI-based video analysis methods and initially support motor tasks such as finger tapping and leg agility. The goal is to make objective motor measurements accessible within a single workflow for both clinicians and researchers. The project aims to:

1. integrate video-based AI analysis into an easy-to-use application;
2. support recording, uploading and analysing Parkinson motor tasks;
3. present objective measurements in a clear and clinically meaningful way;
4. support comparison of motor performance across assessments and conditions;
5. provide workflows that meet both clinical and research needs.

Ultimately, the project aims to create a complete platform in which clinicians and researchers can work with Parkinson motor videos, perform quantitative analysis and use the resulting measurements in one place.
